# Perception and hierarchy review

Reviewer: perception and hierarchy. Brief version 1. Evidence: index of 2026-11-07.

All pixel values below were sampled from the named PNGs with Pillow (2× backing store; I divide by 2 to give points). Every image named under "Observation" was opened and inspected.

## Findings

### PER-1 Failure and warning states carry no more visual weight than "Installed"

- **Observation:** In `01-general-light.png` the right-hand values "Installed", "Passed", "Not checked", "Couldn't load" and "Not connected" are all rendered in the identical secondary gray `rgb(124,124,124)` on the `rgb(247,247,247)` card; the ⚠︎ `exclamationmark.triangle` beside "Couldn't load" is the _same_ gray (sampled at the glyph, 124,124,124). Code confirms it: `app/Runtime/RuntimeCheckRow.swift:15-21` puts the failure triangle and the status text inside one `.foregroundStyle(.secondary)`, and `app/Shared/SettingsValue.swift:11-27` styles every value `.secondary`. Compare `13-scratch-models-set-light.png` with `14-scratch-models-saved-unavailable-light.png`: the healthy footer and the footer reading "New sessions will fail to start until you choose an available model and level" are the same gray (`rgb(128,128,128)` in both, sampled over the same rect), the same size, in the same place — the two panes differ only in words. `16-scratch-models-invalid-file-light.png` shows the raw parse error "$: not JSON: JSON Parse error: Unexpected EOF" in that same caption gray with no icon. Emphasis is also inconsistent for one semantic: General's "Couldn't load" is `.secondary` (`app/Shared/SettingsFileRow.swift:19-20`) while Models' "Couldn't load model settings" is primary black `rgb(38,38,38)` (`02-models-light.png`; `app/Panes/ModelsPane.swift:16-19`). The captures are inactive-window, but chroma is preserved in them — the reveal highlight in `07-reveal-tools-wait-light.png` is fully blue — so the grayness is real, not an artifact of inactivity.
- **Principle/source:** Apple HIG › Color (platform guideline, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color, accessed 2026-11-07): color "communicate[s] status and feedback", and "Apply color sparingly… reserve it for elements that truly benefit from emphasis, such as status indicators". Jeff Johnson, _Designing with the Mind in Mind_ (book, named in this lens' skill): peripheral vision resolves little but is sensitive to color and shape, so a message set in body gray at body size is not noticed until fixated; Gestalt similarity makes identically-styled items read as one undifferentiated class. The fix is bounded by WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 Use of Color (standard, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html, accessed 2026-11-07): color must be additive, never the only cue.
- **User impact:** Core job 1 (confirm Pico is installed and starts, recover when it does not) and job 2 (choose a default model). Demonstrated from captures: a user glancing at `14` versus `13` has no preattentive cue that new sessions will now fail; they must read a two-line caption to find out. Plausible: the same gray makes the Keychain failure in `01` read as just another row value during a scan.
- **Recommendation:** **[polish]** Give the three states distinct, additive encodings while keeping today's words: (a) failures/warnings get a filled system symbol in `Color.red` / `Color.orange` plus the existing text (satisfies 1.4.1: glyph + hue + text); (b) make the two "couldn't load" rows agree — pick General's in-row treatment or Models' full-width row and use it in both; (c) leave benign values `.secondary`. Tradeoff: adds the first chromatic status to a deliberately restrained window and must be re-checked under Increase Contrast and in dark (the HIG supplies increased-contrast red/orange variants); overusing red would flatten the hierarchy again.
- **Severity / confidence:** high / high
- **Validation needed:** Re-capture `01`, `02`, `14`, `16` in light, dark, and Increase Contrast on an _active_ window; confirm the symbol's contrast ≥ 3:1 against the card fill and that VoiceOver still speaks exactly the visible status.

### PER-2 The pane header card is the loudest element on every pane and costs the fold

- **Observation:** In all six panes the first thing in the content area is `PaneHeader` (`app/Panes/PaneHeader.swift`): a 32 pt saturated `AppIconTile` (`app/Shared/AppIconTile.swift`, `destination.tileColor.gradient`) plus title and summary in a full-width card. Measured in `01-general-light.png`: the card runs y 75–123 pt (48 pt tall) with the first real section header ("Runtime") at ~150 pt and the first data row at 185 pt — 133 pt of the viewport elapses before any pane state. The scroll viewport in the captures runs 52 → 810.5 pt (bottom bar divider measured at 810.5 pt). The word "General" appears three times within 250 pt: sidebar row, toolbar title, header card. On `02-models-light.png` and `03-providers-light.png` the purple/blue tile is the only chromatic, highest-saturation object on screen, while the pane's actual state ("Couldn't load model settings", "Not connected") is monochrome. [INFERENCE from the evidence index line "the app's default height is 680 pt"]: at the default height the viewport is ~589 pt, so visible content ends near y ≈ 641 pt — General's whole Versions group (measured 624–728 pt) and its footer, and the last row of Models' Available models card (354.5–671.5 pt in `13`), fall below the fold; the header card plus its spacing accounts for ~75 pt of that loss.
- **Principle/source:** Apple HIG › Settings, macOS (platform guideline, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings, accessed 2026-11-07): "Update the window's title to reflect the currently visible pane" — the pane name is already the window/toolbar title, so the card is a third copy. Apple HIG › Color (accessed 2026-11-07): "Apply color sparingly… reserve it for elements that truly benefit from emphasis." Johnson: salience should track task importance; saturated color is a pop-out channel and spending it on a static decoration mis-ranks the page.
- **User impact:** Every job, every pane. Demonstrated: the title is triplicated and the state-bearing rows start 133 pt down. Plausible (not tested at 680 pt): at the default window size the user must scroll to reach content that would otherwise fit.
- **Recommendation:** **[polish]** Compress the header to a single secondary-styled summary line directly beneath the section's first heading (or as the first section's footer), dropping the card chrome, the 32 pt tile and the repeated title; keep the `.isHeader` trait at the top of the pane so the VoiceOver heading at `01-general-light.ax.txt:41` survives. Tradeoff: the panes lose their one spot of color and the colored tile that visually ties a pane to its sidebar row — acceptable because the sidebar tile still carries that mapping, and PER-1 wants the chroma budget spent on status. Deleting the summary text outright would be **[proposal]**, since it removes something a user can read today.
- **Severity / confidence:** medium / high (geometry measured; fold effect inferred from the index's stated default height)
- **Validation needed:** Build at the 680 pt default and capture all six panes before/after; confirm General fits without scrolling and that the AX heading still opens each pane.

### PER-3 The trailing value column is mostly repeated boilerplate, so real values do not stand out

- **Observation:** `04-tools-light.png`: eight rows each ending "Included" under the heading "Included tools", then three ending "Excluded" under "Excluded tools" — 11 right-aligned gray words restating their own section headers, forming the strongest vertical pattern on the pane and competing with the tool names, which are the only varying data. Across the app the same column repeats "Not connected" 15 times: `01` (3 Versions rows), `04` (intellisense), `05-sesh-light.png` (5), `06-diagnostics-light.png` (5), `03` (1). All of it is the same `.secondary` gray as the genuinely informative values ("Installed", "Passed", "gpt-oss-20b"), per `app/Shared/SettingsValue.swift:11-27`. Dark behaves identically (`42-tools-dark.png`, `46-diagnostics-dark.png`; status text sampled `rgb(157,157,157)` on `rgb(37,37,37)` in `40-scratch-general-dark.png`).
- **Principle/source:** Tufte, _The Visual Display of Quantitative Information_ (book, named in this lens' skill): maximize the share of ink that carries data; erase redundant ink. Johnson/Gestalt similarity and proximity: a column of identical tokens is perceived as one texture, and a lone different token inside it loses the pop-out it would otherwise have.
- **User impact:** Job 4 (understand the fixed tool policy) and job 1 (read General at a glance). Demonstrated: on Tools the policy is encoded twice, and on General the three dead Versions rows occupy 104 pt of card directly below the settings-file row that actually failed. Plausible: a user scanning General's value column for the failure has to discriminate it from four other gray phrases.
- **Recommendation:** **[polish]** (a) Tools: merge "Included tools" and "Excluded tools" into one "Built-in tools" group so Included/Excluded becomes the column's data rather than a restatement — the row values and their AX values are preserved. (b) Keep "Not connected" on every scaffold row (hard rule) but style it one step quieter than live values — e.g. tertiary label plus the same wrench glyph used in the bottom bar — so live status wins the comparison while the honesty marker stays visible and spoken. Tradeoff: (a) loses the at-a-glance block of three excluded tools (mitigated by keeping them last and by a leading glyph); (b) tertiary gray is lower-contrast, so it must be checked against Increase Contrast rather than dropped below legibility.
- **Severity / confidence:** medium / high
- **Validation needed:** Re-capture `04`, `05`, `06` after the change; verify each row's AX value is unchanged (`04-tools-light.ax.txt`), and measure the tertiary text's contrast in both appearances and with Increase Contrast on.

### PER-4 The saved default is marked by a word 318 pt away from the model it marks

- **Observation:** `13-scratch-models-set-light.png`: in Available models, "gpt-oss-20b" ends at x ≈ 321 pt and its marker "Default" begins at x ≈ 639 pt — about 318 pt of empty row between them — and the marker is the same `.secondary` gray as every other value (`app/Panes/ModelRows.swift:88-91` renders it with `SettingsValueText`). Six rows are otherwise visually identical (name on line 1, slug on line 2), so the one row that differs differs only by a distant gray word; in dark (`41-scratch-models-set-dark.png`) the same arrangement holds. In `11-scratch-models-not-set-light.png` the model that new sessions will actually use — `lm-studio/qwen/qwen3-coder-30b`, named in the footer — carries no marker at all in the list, while the Model row reads "Not Set".
- **Principle/source:** Gestalt proximity (Johnson, _Designing with the Mind in Mind_): a label is bound to the item nearest it; separating a marker from its referent by a near-empty span weakens that binding. Apple HIG › Color (accessed 2026-11-07): reserve emphasis for the element that benefits — here, the current selection. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 (accessed 2026-11-07) keeps the text label mandatory if a glyph or tint is added.
- **User impact:** Core job 2. Demonstrated: after choosing a default, confirming _which_ row is current requires scanning a six-row list's far-right edge rather than one fixation. Plausible: in the not-set state the user cannot tell from the list which model is in force.
- **Recommendation:** **[polish]** Mark the default row with a leading accent checkmark adjacent to the model name (keeping the text "Default" for VoiceOver, moved next to the name or retained as the row's accessibility value), so the marker sits within the name's proximity group. Tradeoff: slight duplication with the Model picker above; leading glyphs add a 20 pt gutter to all rows, which lengthens the card. Marking the fallback row in the not-set state would be **[proposal]** — it tells the user something the list does not say today.
- **Severity / confidence:** medium / medium (positions measured; the attention cost is inferred from proximity, not tested)
- **Validation needed:** Re-capture `11`, `13`, `14`; confirm VoiceOver still announces the default row distinctly and that the marker stays visible with Increase Contrast.

### PER-5 The same action lands in four different places, so there is no learnable "where the button is"

- **Observation:** Measured button extents (light captures): in `12-scratch-general-file-exists-light.png` "Show in Finder…" sits **inside** the Settings file card, trailing, x 562.5–684 pt; in `16-scratch-models-invalid-file-light.png` the same-labeled "Show in Finder…" sits **outside** the card, **leading**, left edge ≈ 244 pt, below two lines of footer text. In `01-general-light.png` "Try Again" is inside the row, trailing (x 604.5–684 pt), while "Check Runtime"/"Check for Updates" are inside the card on their own row, leading-aligned at x 245 pt. In `03-providers-light.png` the disabled "Add Provider…" is outside the card, trailing, vertically centered against a two-line footer caption, whereas in `05-sesh-light.png` the equivalent disabled "Register" is inside the row, trailing. So one app shows four placements (inside-trailing, inside-leading, outside-trailing, outside-leading) for the same class of control.
- **Principle/source:** Gestalt similarity and common region (Johnson, _Designing with the Mind in Mind_): placing like controls in unlike regions prevents a stable expectation from forming, so each screen must be searched afresh; controls inside a card read as belonging to that row, controls beside a footer read as belonging to the caption. Apple HIG › Settings, macOS (accessed 2026-11-07) stresses a stable settings interface that "help[s] people find what they need".
- **User impact:** Jobs 1 and 3 (recover the runtime, find the settings file) and job 2's recovery path. Demonstrated: the identical "Show in Finder…" action occupies two different regions and alignments depending on pane and state.
- **Recommendation:** **[polish]** Pick one rule and apply it everywhere — row-scoped actions trailing inside their row ("Try Again", "Show in Finder…", "Register"), section-scoped actions on their own trailing-aligned row at the bottom of the card ("Check Runtime", "Check for Updates", "Add Provider…") — and never align a button against footer caption text. Tradeoff: moving "Add Provider…" into the card gives a disabled control more prominence; keeping it disabled and gray preserves the honesty rule, and the brief's bottom bar still reports the pane's unconnected state.
- **Severity / confidence:** medium / high
- **Validation needed:** Re-capture `01`, `03`, `05`, `12`, `16` after unification and check keyboard tab order still reaches each button in reading order.

## Worth keeping

- **The reveal highlight.** `07-reveal-tools-wait-light.png` and `43-reveal-shell-dark.png`: the blue outline plus tinted fill around the Wait row is shape-and-color, survives the inactive-window appearance, and is the clearest emphasis in the whole app. It is the model the status work in PER-1 should follow.
- **Row grammar.** Label left, value right, explanatory caption as a section footer, 20 pt sidebar tiles — consistent across all six panes and in both appearances (`01`/`40`, `04`/`42`, `06`/`46`). It makes the panes feel like one app and should not be traded away for per-pane layouts.
- **Verbatim things look verbatim.** The monospaced `pico` and `intellisense location` labels (`01`, `04`) and the middle-truncated path in the Installation row distinguish literal identifiers from prose without extra chrome.
- **Sidebar grouping is real and System Settings-like.** Measured 32 pt row pitch within a group and 45 pt across group boundaries in `01-general-light.png`, matching `SettingsDestination.sidebarGroups`; the gaps are large enough to read as groups without rules or headers. Keep the gray General tile — System Settings uses gray for General too.

## Lens score

6/10 — rubric: (1) does hierarchy rank the pane's primary job and current state first; (2) do related items group and unrelated items separate; (3) is emphasis contrast proportionate to importance; (4) typography and alignment consistency; (5) does the encoding read correctly in both appearances. The app scores well on (2) and (4): grouping, row grammar and sidebar spacing are measurably System Settings-like and hold up in dark.
